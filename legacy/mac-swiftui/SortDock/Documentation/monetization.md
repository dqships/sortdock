# Monetization

## Recommended model

SortDock should be a one-time purchase, not a subscription.

It is a compact, local macOS utility. It does not require an account, cloud storage, or an ongoing hosted service. The purchase promise should be simple:

> Buy once. Your files stay on your Mac.

Launch SortDock Pro at $9.99. Raise the price to $12.99 to $14.99 after the app has earned strong reviews and a proven track record of reliable updates.

Do not use ads, sell user data, or introduce cloud upsells that conflict with the app's local-first privacy promise.

## Free tier

The free version should be permanently useful. It lets people build the basic sorting habit without making the app feel broken.

- Watch one folder, normally Downloads.
- Create up to three destination folders.
- Add up to ten file-type routing rules.
- Choose Ask Before Moving or Move Automatically.
- Keep conflict-safe naming and basic activity history.

The free setup covers common routes such as Downloads to Images, PDFs, and Archives.

## Pro unlock

SortDock Pro is a one-time $9.99 purchase that unlocks the complete set-it-once workflow.

- Unlimited watched folders, destination folders, and routing rules.
- Keyword-based routing, such as sending files with "invoice" in the name to Finance.
- Custom action delays and Ask Later settings.
- Run at login and advanced routing choices.
- Future Pro updates.

## Why this fits

People pay for reliable, quiet automation that saves them from repeatedly cleaning up Downloads or maintaining scripts. The strongest reasons to upgrade are custom rules, keyword routing, flexible timing, reliable background operation, and safe duplicate handling.

A subscription would not match the product's small, private, local-first nature. Keep the paid offer easy to understand: the free app handles a few common routes; Pro handles everything the customer wants to automate.

## Future options

Only consider a paid major-version upgrade when it includes meaningful new value, such as cross-Mac rule sync, shared rule libraries, or substantially more powerful workflows. Do not add recurring billing unless SortDock gains a real ongoing service that customers choose to use.
